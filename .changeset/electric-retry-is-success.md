A stream error that `onError` retries no longer fails the initial sync or the subset loads that are waiting for it. The collection stays `loading` while `onError` decides, and waiting loads settle when the retried stream delivers its snapshot. Before, the collection entered `error` and waiting loads rejected even when the retry succeeded, which could leave a joined or included live query in error for good. A stream error without `onError`, or one where `onError` gives up or throws, still fails the initial sync and its waiting loads. The initial sync also fails when the Electric client gives up by itself after `onError` asked for a retry, for example on a response without Electric headers or after its retry limit, and when the stream's external `signal` aborts before the first ready. Before, the collection stayed `loading` in both cases. When the initial sync fails, waiting loads and readiness waits such as `preload()` now reject with the same cause, including when `onError` throws. A status listener that cleans the Collection up while it enters `error` no longer makes a waiting load succeed without rows. Once the first up-to-date is committed, its persistence step alone decides the initial sync: the Collection becomes ready when that snapshot is accepted, even after a retry or a give-up, and fails with the persistence error when it is not.

Migration note: while `onError` decides whether to retry, the Collection now stays `loading`, and `preload()` and other readiness waits stay pending. Before, they entered `error` and rejected at once, even when the retry later succeeded. Code that showed a reconnecting state on `status === 'error'` during a retry should read `onError` itself instead.
