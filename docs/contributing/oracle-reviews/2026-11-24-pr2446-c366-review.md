## Laws and experiments

### Storage access requires current source authority

The cache-claim contract and glossary distinguish an expired claim from a live
claim's I/O failure. An old source commit must abort and schedule scoped recovery
when its authority expires. An unexpected I/O error under a live claim remains
terminal. A successful receipt also owes the complete public and durable row.

The prior FIFO and managed-claim owners tested these concerns separately. They
did not combine source commits diverted by hydration with claim expiry. The
expanded owner crosses ordinary, diverted and queued commits with expiry before
and during baseline access, recovery during renewal, and expiry between promise
continuations. The latter histories found another production defect: a check
inside an async helper still leaves a continuation before application.

The final repair shares rejection/recovery logic and checks currentness
synchronously at the application continuation. It includes known expiry,
lifecycle, storage identity and pending recovery. Expired baseline errors use
the same existing recovery policy. The repair removes a redundant baseline
guard, async catch machinery and an impossible empty-queue branch.

Independent review also repaired two observation gaps. A next receipt alone
did not establish that recovery accepted new data. The comparison now checks
the next public row, durable row and current storage target. Completed
observations also survive a later read failure. The actual SQLite mutant that
acknowledges a write but drops its row fails the durable-value comparison.

The [dedicated record](2026-11-24-persisted-baseline-claims.md) contains the
pre-edit controlled/Node SQLite/Chromium OPFS failures, final source bindings,
990 broader Node checks, 129 browser checks, fifteen required cuts and the
final nine-operator report. Each cut reaches at least ten constructed histories
per campaign and receiving path. Source mutants reject the original behavior,
an early-only guard, an async-tail-only guard, omitted expiry, removed admission
and acknowledged-but-lost future writes. Setup failures and a timed-out old
loop mutant remain distinct from named-law kills.

### Native confirmation belongs to the writing Collection

Authority is the native transactor's documented observation obligation, its
existing staged-sync-transaction contract, the PowerSync guide and the
optimistic settlement law. A mutation cannot retire its optimistic state before
its writing Collection stages the confirming source update. An outstanding
confirmation rejects when its sync run stops. A peer or replacement cannot revive it.
This does not promise cancellation or rollback of committed SQLite effects.

The decoder owner explicitly excluded real native mutation handlers. The hook
owner used a controlled optimistic hold and never entered the native pending
operation store. The first new real-host campaigns found 28 law failures and
34 passing neighbors on each host. Pending handlers survived fatal decoding
and cleanup. A healthy same-table peer could later fulfill those retired waits.
The review overstated the other timing: mutations submitted after complete
tracking disposal already rejected through SQLite rather than all hanging.

Captured run signals now reject outstanding waits and isolate retired runs.
Independent review found an introduced rejection window while the actual SDK
forwarded a completed transaction result. The aggregate now owns its rejection
immediately while preserving the original rejected outcome.

A further generated neighbor keeps both Collections healthy and releases their
callbacks in either order. Originally, a peer could fulfill the writer's handler
while the writer's base was empty. Its publications showed optimistic row,
empty result, then the source row. Twelve peer-first units failed on each host;
twelve owner-first neighbors passed. The final resolver matches the observing
run with each waiter's captured run. Legacy signal-less store callers retain
their existing behavior.

The final mutation owner passes 196 checks per host. All 47 states reach at
least five generated histories in each fixed/random campaign on Node 22 and
Chromium. The final native neighbors pass 136 checks. An earlier source phase
also passes 1,213 Node checks, 826 browser decoder/mutation checks and 253
browser hook checks. Those broader counts precede the active-peer filter and
are not presented as one final-tree command.

Three isolated wrong designs fail on both hosts: first-table owner lookup,
an unobserved aggregate rejection, and removal of the observing-run filter.
The last fails all twelve selected peer-first comparisons per host. Actual SQL
contents remain diagnostics. The held-return premise is a controlled SDK
method-return hold after real SQL, not a claim about a naturally stalled COMMIT.

### Partial construction releases its resources

The cleanup/acquisition contract and LIFE-2/LIFE-10 require release of resources
already accepted before startup fails. The old driver supplied only valid
streams and observed resources after sync returned successfully.

The new shared construction judgment varies failure stage, refused SDK values,
subscription position and a surviving ready peer. Real SDK normalization
refuses empty names, BigInt, circular parameters and throwing `toJSON` values.
One-shot collaborator faults occur before that collaborator accepts a new
resource. They do not assert cleanup of unknown SDK-internal allocations.

Unchanged production gives 26 failed and 20 passing construction units per
host. The repair invokes existing cleanup and rethrows the original synchronous
startup error. The final comparison checks resources after the first and
second public cleanup calls, including the healthy peer's delivery and release.
Independent review caught the initial omission of the second cleanup cut.

Final construction: 49 checks per host. The cleanup-removal source mutant fails
the intended resource comparison on both hosts. Bound controls reject primary
error replacement, peer release and duplicate release. No extra lifecycle or
recovery state was added.

### Connection and stream failures have independent lifetimes

This required a product decision. The old model explicitly kept a connection
outage until every retained stream recovered. Public wording did not uniquely
choose that rule. On 2026-11-24 the maintainer chose to clear a recovered
connection cause while preserving a still-failing stream's cause.

Guide, model, history grammar, failure-frame comparison and production now
express that decision. Actual SDK `ready` establishes reconnection. Later
outages add a new cause and cannot resurrect the old one. A failed stream stays
visible until its own recovery or release.

Before production edits, shared-client campaigns give 163 failed/136 passing
units per host under the new law. The five on-demand receiving paths give
180 failed/60 passing Node units and 120 failed/40 passing browser units.
These include clean-subject calibration failures, not just semantic failures.
No setup error or timeout supplies the RED evidence.

Final shared client: 299 checks per host. Final targeted health paths: 240 Node
checks across raw/controlled/SQLite paths and 160 browser checks across raw/OPFS.
Existing request-boundary neighbors pass 71 checks per host. A source mutant
that clears the entire health frame loses the stream cause and fails on both
hosts. Exact SDK AbortError identity remains diagnostic; category, rows and
ownership remain strict.

### TrailBase uses fresh evidence and rejects impossible confirmation

The automatic mutation/optimistic contract requires a new source event. An old
same-key event cannot establish success, including within one clock tick. The
original review author agrees that main's immediate success after stream end
was false confirmation.

The initial terminal-stream extension allowed truthful immediate or deadline
rejection because no prompt-rejection policy had authority. The maintainer
then explicitly chose prompt rejection on 2026-11-24. This supersedes that
permitted alternative without retroactively treating the earlier model as
wrong. The fresh-event rule remains.

The stricter shared owner gives forty comparison failures and twenty passing
neighbors on each actual SDK host before production changes. No setup failure
or test timeout supplies these results. Histories cover CRUD, eager/on-demand,
EOF/error, pending confirmation and known-terminal admission, buffered graceful
EOF, retirement/replacement, and a healthy missing-event deadline.

The same grammar also reaches a separate existing defect. A fresh event can
arrive while the backend response stays pending. The SDK has no CRUD HTTP
timeout and supports custom transports. The old periodic cleanup discards that
event after five minutes. When the successful response later arrives, the
handler forgets established proof and waits for another event. The receiver
executes the actual prune callback under controlled time. This is proof loss,
not a proposed guarantee to cancel a slow backend request.

The repair replaces the historical event cache, Store dependency and periodic
pruning timer with request-scoped collectors owned by the captured sync run.
It keeps established proof while HTTP is pending and releases collectors when
the handler settles. Missing proof rejects once the response is available and
the run is stopped. A healthy missing-event wait retains its ordinary deadline.

A new public-callback grammar exposed a defect in the first collector repair:
the event's publication could synchronously start a new handler, which the live
collector loop then notified using that older event. Fifteen comparisons failed
on each real host before the cohort repair. The listener now snapshots eligible
collectors before parsing and publishing the event, then notifies that snapshot.
This preserves event-before-entry ordering without a historical token cache.

The final six-file Node run passes 1,034 checks. The actual Chromium mutation
receiver passes all 569. Twenty-seven states each have five constructed builders;
each builder reaches five histories in both fixed and fresh campaigns on each
host. The original 60-history campaign budgets remain. A guarded seed/path
replay of the reentrant insert passes. Scoped types pass. After a comment and
restoration of the prior multi-ID timeout error detail, the healthy-deadline
selection also passes with no type error.

Four isolated source controls fail the shared comparison on both hosts:
omitted terminal abort at `response-without-confirmation`, discarded established
proof at `both-obligations`, omitted healthy timeout at `confirmation-deadline`,
and a live collector loop at `first-obligation`. Each has one selected law
failure and one passing neighbor. Source-load markers establish the variant
actually ran. Three initial Node attempts reused Vite's transform cache, and
one subsequent selection matched no test. Those attempts supply no mutant
evidence. Fresh caches and corrected selections supply the reported failures.
The independent reviewer inspected all eight final host/control results.

The law does not cancel an in-flight backend HTTP request or promise backend
rollback. A fresh event accepted before closure remains useful when the
response follows. Graceful EOF must drain a buffered valid event before
confirmation becomes impossible. A retired run cannot borrow a new run's
event. The primary oracle owns these crossings; an isolated timeout example
does not close them.

## Reviewer assessment

Hire for review hypothesis generation, with executable verification required
before closure. The reviewer identified important claim, waiter, resource and
release failures. It also exposed a real build boundary through a structural
comment. Source analysis was useful, but several effects were overstated or
depended on an unproved schedule. Prioritizing the open internal transaction
above its missing public counterexample was weak. The follow-up correctly
narrowed TrailBase's allegation and acknowledged the unconfirmed Direct case.

The most valuable omissions were interacting actors and observation cuts:
ordinary versus hydration admission, real native handlers versus decoder
refusal, healthy observers versus stopped peers, and build outputs versus
workspace source. More samples of the old grammars could not supply those
distinctions.
