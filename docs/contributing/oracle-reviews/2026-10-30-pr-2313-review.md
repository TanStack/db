## Oracle repair and measured reach

The governing row model and exact comparisons are unchanged. Settle commands
can now supply explicit row data, independently of the fixture's attempt ID.
Generated values range from -100,000 to 100,000. Zero to three preliminary
retired acquisitions perturb numbering before the tested restart.

The single scenario grammar supplies both history construction and declared
reach. A separate observer classifies executed owner operations, adapter
acquisition counts, actual settlement, and callback-applied versus written rows.
Rejected acquisitions establish no rows. Their successful retry supplies the
row-relation witness, after the actual rejection is observed. A canceled or
absent acquisition claims neither restored equality nor a rejection.

- Acquisition delivery: 24 cases, each measured 12 times per campaign.
- Independent source delivery: 27 cases, each measured 12 times per campaign.
- Direct, filtered, ordered-snapshot, and snapshot-before-source-events receivers:
  three row relations each, measured 12 times per campaign and receiving path.
- Fixed and random campaigns share the generator, budget, recorder and comparison.
  Together these campaigns execute 1,512 restart histories at multiplier 1.

The old 72-label count included observationally redundant canceled acquisition
relationships and no-op outcomes. Removing those duplicate categories does not
remove a valid publication distinction. Every generated-input budget remains
12. The original broad fixed/random budgets remain 60 each. O5's recommended
24-input enumeration would itself run 24 × 72 = 1,728 histories, the old total.
It would improve finite input enumeration, but would not reduce that work.
The revised grammar spends its budget on distinct structural cases and a much
larger payload domain. This is no elapsed-performance claim.

The receiving driver uses the existing independent Map diff. A real predicate
excludes b. An ordered snapshot selects the whole bounded two-row replacement.
A held source publication installs rows before `requestSnapshot()` reads them,
then releases its source events before acquisition settlement. The held-cut
comparison checks unchanged subscriber rows and no callbacks. The settled-cut
comparison checks exact batches, values, omitted-row deletion and readiness.
No test discards installed source events to invent an unsupported public path.
