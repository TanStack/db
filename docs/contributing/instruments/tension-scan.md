# Tension scan

**Use when:** individually sensible demands appear to collide, or new evidence
may have changed an earlier conflict.

**Inputs:** the obligations and their sources, the condition in which they
interact, and any earlier formulation. Record which statements are accepted
contracts, user goals, current behavior, or your own inferences.

## Operation

1. Establish why each demand matters independently. If only one has support,
   return that asymmetry rather than manufacturing two equally sound sides.
2. State the collision concretely: we need A and B, but under C, satisfying A
   undermines B through mechanism M. Work through a legal history or design
   consequence; a pair of opposing slogans is insufficient.
3. Look for a missing distinction in scope, authority, ordering, ownership, or
   checkpoint. Recompute the consequences with that distinction explicit.
4. Check whether both original obligations survive. Removing a guarantee can
   be a proposed tradeoff, but does not demonstrate that the tension dissolved.
5. On a recheck, identify the evidence that makes the tension remain live,
   sharpen, move, weaken, or dissolve. Read it against the original purpose;
   recent or vivid material alone does not justify changing the question.

**Return:** the sourced demands, concrete collision and mechanism, possible
missing distinction, and current status; or no supported tension, with the
missing support identified. Preserve materially different interpretations when
the available authority cannot choose among them.

**Control:** restate the case without an A-versus-B framing. Does the conflict
still exist? Keep a generated collision separate from an observed one.

**Distortion:** invented symmetry, apparent resolution by deleting an obligation,
or using an ordinary clarification to claim a major design discovery.

**Stop when:** the collision or its dissolution is demonstrated at the stated
scope, or a specific missing fact or design decision is identified.
