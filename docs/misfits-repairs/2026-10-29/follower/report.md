## Law, authority and test gap

Issue #2139's maintainer design and #2128's applied-settlement law require a fresh rotated follower to hold owner notices until every retained demand reacquires. Success ends scoped recovery and applies rows; a crossing load waits for those rows. A retained failure keeps quarantine and reports a remote failure. Caller release or cancellation removes its result obligation. A provider's error name does not retire a retained demand.

The unchanged shared model/check already expressed this distinction. The old grammar missed it: it supplied `AbortError` only after release, so it never challenged name-based retirement. The new separate generated lane crosses ordinary Error, Error named AbortError, and undefined rejection independently with retained failure and actual release/abort. Its generated axes also vary reply order, owner notice between replies, a/b/both failure, new demand c, and source-triggered versus two-read claim-loss rotation. Each generated input executes all three representations in both retention forms through the same driver and judgment. A fixed replay of the reviewer's active AbortError+c history also uses that shared judgment, without a separate expected answer.

The superseding-leader c observation had no accepted policy authority (C13). Its exact literal expectation was moved unchanged into ordinary `persisted.test.ts`. The shared controlled production driver was extracted into `follower-rotation-support.ts`; its accepted-law comparisons did not change. No generated axis or run budget was removed. Bounded failed-reacquisition retry policy (C12) and superseding-leader new-demand policy (C13) remain decisions.
