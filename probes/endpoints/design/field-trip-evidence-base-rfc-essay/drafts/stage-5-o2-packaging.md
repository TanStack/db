## Frozen plain baseline

TanStack Trust is a proposed reusable base for domain-embedded software trust
systems. A domain package defines the claim, rules, checks, adequate evidence,
and applicability. The base records and evaluates explicit evidence routes,
preserves provenance, freshness, contradiction, and repair history, and exposes
that state through common interfaces. Consumer policy decides permission and
fallback. The base does not make an agent, change, checker, formal model, or
domain claim trustworthy by itself.

The current implementation is a bounded local prototype with TanStack DB
Endpoints as one worked consumer. Generic package authoring, richer
applicability, checker and encoding soundness, agent-workflow effectiveness,
installed clients, independent range, production operation, and outcome
improvement remain open.

## P1 — The direct product question

### Title

**Can We Trust What Agents Produce?**

### Subtitle

**A TanStack Trust RFC for domain-owned guarantees, reusable evidence, and
consumer-owned decisions**

### Description

Coding agents can produce changes faster than maintainers can justify relying
on them. This RFC proposes TanStack Trust: common machinery that lets each
software domain define its own promises and checks, retain the evidence and
counterevidence behind them, and expose that record to humans and agents
without turning it into a universal trust verdict.

### Frame reading

- **Foregrounds:** Kyle's product question and the economic gap between cheap
  generation and expensive reliance.
- **Claim/confidence:** Poses the broad question, then supplies a bounded
  architectural answer; it must deny a general yes/no answer in the opening.
- **Causal relation:** Trust makes grounds inspectable; it does not demonstrate
  that agent output improves or becomes safe.
- **Scope/source role:** All domains remain hypothetical beyond the bounded
  Endpoints consumer. AIUC supplies adjacent problem language only.
- **Expected-basin overlap:** Resembles an AI-safety or agent-certification
  essay before the subtitle and opening establish the software-domain boundary.
- **Source-specific remainder preserved:** The title retains Kyle's exact
  motivating question and creates room to explain why the answer decomposes
  into domain meaning, evidence state, reach, and consumer policy.
- **Chief loss/risk:** The interrogative can imply that TanStack Trust evaluates
  agents or returns a scalar answer. An extracted title without its subtitle is
  especially vulnerable to overarching-product drift.

## P2 — The product and authority contract

### Title

**TanStack Trust: A Base for Domain-Owned Software Guarantees**

### Subtitle

**Separating domain meaning, reusable evidence mechanics, and consumer policy**

### Description

TanStack Trust is a proposed base layer for building evidence systems inside
specific software domains. Domain packages own what their guarantees mean and
which checks have authority; the base maintains inspectable support,
contradiction, freshness, and repair records; consumers decide what that state
permits. This RFC defines those handoffs, shows the bounded Endpoints vertical,
and identifies what remains unimplemented or undecided.

### Frame reading

- **Foregrounds:** The O2 authority map and the exact product boundary.
- **Claim/confidence:** States a proposed product category directly while
  retaining the bounded status in the description.
- **Causal relation:** Describes ownership and maintenance mechanics rather
  than implying measured improvement.
- **Scope/source role:** Makes domain locality explicit in the title; Endpoints
  remains a worked consumer rather than evidence of generality.
- **Expected-basin overlap:** Resembles a conventional architecture RFC or
  platform-layer proposal.
- **Source-specific remainder preserved:** Carries the domain/base/consumer seam
  most accurately and gives formal-system donors a clear subordinate role as
  domain-owned check forms and typed observations.
- **Chief loss/risk:** “Base” and “software guarantees” can sound more complete,
  formal, and production-ready than the prototype. It gives the cheap-code
  problem less emotional force and may feel architectural before it feels
  necessary.

## P3 — The handoff from generation to reliance

### Title

**From Generated Code to Justified Reliance**

### Subtitle

**The domain, evidence, and policy handoffs behind TanStack Trust**

### Description

Generating code is becoming cheap; maintaining defensible reasons to rely on a
change is not. This RFC follows the responsibilities that no generic agent or
base layer can collapse: a domain defines the promise and its checks, TanStack
Trust preserves and explains the resulting evidence, and each consumer decides
how to act. The Endpoints prototype shows one bounded vertical and the work
still required to make the pattern agent-operable.

### Frame reading

- **Foregrounds:** The movement between owners and the difference between
  production capability and justified use.
- **Claim/confidence:** Names the desired transition while the description
  limits the system to maintaining defensible reasons.
- **Causal relation:** The word “to” can imply a pipeline that produces trust;
  the body must show that reliance is a consumer judgment supported, not
  caused, by the base.
- **Scope/source role:** “Generated code” centers agent-produced changes but may
  understate human-authored changes and non-code software artifacts.
- **Expected-basin overlap:** Resembles a transformation narrative or product
  journey more than an architecture RFC.
- **Source-specific remainder preserved:** Best retains O2's handoffs while
  giving the cheap-code thesis forward motion.
- **Chief loss/risk:** It can promise an achieved outcome, narrow the product to
  AI-generated code, and make the base appear to bridge the entire gap from
  generation to permission.

## Shared packaging limits

No option may imply that TanStack Trust:

- certifies an agent, change, organization, or domain package;
- proves a registered rule, checker, solver encoding, oracle, or model-to-code
  relation sound;
- reduces evidence, execution/reach, and consumer policy to one score;
- contains a universal solver or formal logic;
- has demonstrated independent range beyond its Endpoints-shaped lineage;
- has measured human or agent workflow effectiveness; or
- is production-ready or known to improve generated software.
