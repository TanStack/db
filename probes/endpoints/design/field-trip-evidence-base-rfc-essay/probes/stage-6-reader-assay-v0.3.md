## Stable reconstruction

All three readers recovered:

- Trust as an internal Endpoints architecture for bounded, domain-owned
  software guarantees rather than a score, certifier, or standalone product;
- the domain-package → Trust evidence lifecycle → consumer-policy authority
  split, with workflows and CLI/LSP/MCP as carriers;
- AND premises within a route and OR across alternate routes;
- observations evaluated through reach, applicability, freshness,
  contradiction, repair, and expiry;
- repair as a causally later, applicable replay of the same law and case rather
  than any later green result;
- the finite local kernel, verified local store, one Endpoints check, and shared
  service adapters as a bounded vertical;
- the distinction among present, proposed/absent, unresolved, unselected, and
  unmeasured work; and
- no demonstrated rule soundness, other-domain range, agent effectiveness, or
  shipping permission.

Two readers explicitly reconstructed all four state questions—evidence,
production reach, operational failure, and consumer action. The third kept
reach and policy separate in its lifecycle and correctly located durable
operational-failure storage among the unresolved work.

## Stable memory and usable change

Every reader named exact causal replay among the three details that remained
most clearly. All three also retained support-versus-permission; two named the
AND/OR route shape in their top three and the third used it in the recovered
system map. Two named the four state axes in their top three; the other used
reach explicitly in its lifecycle.

The generated usable changes were also consistent:

- inspect a green result by asking which claim and route it supports, whether
  production was reached, which dependencies were captured, what remains
  omitted, and who may act;
- explain why a green check or closed task is insufficient;
- trace an unresolved claim through alternate routes, current context,
  challenges, replay, and expiry;
- sketch or review a domain package for scope, an independent oracle, reach and
  fault controls, omissions, applicability inputs, and replay obligation; and
- organize Endpoints implementation around missing semantic operations rather
  than adapter count.

These are generated statements of possible use, not observations of a human
maintainer completing those tasks.

## Genericity signals

All three described several components as familiar: one service behind several
adapters, provenance and versioning, append-only history, self-certification
warnings, formal-method boundaries, file-persistence caveats, fault-oriented
testing, security and scale gaps, and a staged build sequence.

All three located the architecture-specific combination in the same region:
AND/OR routes, production reach separated from findings, byte-identical
non-revival, causal same-case replay, and the domain/Trust/consumer authority
split. This is a response pattern, not a novelty claim.

## Unsupported or corrected reader inference

- Two readers inferred general challenge-to-route defeat semantics stronger
  than the RFC specifies.
- Two initially expanded “production-shaped” toward deployed execution; the
  RFC establishes only local reach through an extracted helper and excludes
  real PostgreSQL behavior.
- One inferred manual or hard-coded rule admission; admission governance is
  merely absent.
- One inferred every test and mutation control's individual outcome; the RFC
  reports aggregate bounded evidence without the full ledger.
- One inferred a future three-valued applicability result's propagation into
  assessment; it remains undecided.
- One inferred a durable task dependency graph; only gap projection is
  proposed.
- One inferred a runtime location for consumer policy; the RFC assigns
  authority, not placement.
- One inferred unresolved outcomes never open challenges and one inferred a
  rule may support several claims; neither universal behavior is specified.

The apparent `@tanstack/trust`, cryptographic-producer, and standalone-product
inferences were explicitly corrected by the readers after the body stated the
opposite.

## Comparison with the earlier reader promise

The three model responses match the earlier bounded prediction in four ways:

1. they recover who owns meaning, evidence mechanics, carriers, and action;
2. they trace how support can become stale, contradicted, repaired, and expired
   without confusing non-reach or consumer denial with evidence;
3. they distinguish implemented interfaces from absent generic operations and
   unresolved semantics; and
4. they describe concrete inspection, check-design, explanation, and
   implementation tasks they could now attempt.

The earlier promise did not offer correctness, settled applicability, a
prioritized roadmap, production readiness, or demonstrated agent
effectiveness. The readers did not recover those as results. They treated the
build order as proposed dependency structure, not settled priority.

This comparison tests the promise against three generated reader samples. It
does not show that Kyle or another human maintainer will receive the same value.

## Limits

All three readers share model lineage and read under the same technical profile.
Their agreement is correlated and cannot estimate an audience distribution.
The detailed question set may increase recovery of system relations, although
it did not name the desired answers. Actual maintainer comprehension, memory
after delay, disagreement, and ability to use the interfaces remain
unmeasured.
