## Raw response

### Likely reading

1. **Ownership.** The “domain package defines the guarantee,” including “rules and checks,” applicability, and omissions. “TanStack Trust maintains the evidence graph and its history.” “Consumer policy decides permission, severity, fallback, and deployment action.”

2. **Mechanism and time.** Bounded checks produce “observations” that travel through “support routes,” then are evaluated for “applicability” and “freshness.” Later “contradictions, repair,” or changed conditions can invalidate them—the governing question is “what would make that justification expire?”

3. **Roles.** Agents and people perform authoring and repair through “CLI / LSP / MCP” carriers over “one service.” These workflows “do not acquire authority of their own,” and the architecture avoids “letting an agent approve its own claims.” Consumers alone “decide use,” “own fallback,” and “apply policy.”

4. **Product shape and problem.** Trust is an “internal architecture and evidence contract,” shipped “inside TanStack DB Endpoints,” for automating guarantee work that is “manual today” while separating “domain meaning, reusable evidence mechanics, and consumer policy.”

5. **What exists now.** The worktree has “a finite local kernel, verified file storage, one Endpoints check, and CLI, LSP, and MCP adapters over one service”—explicitly “a useful vertical slice.”

6. **What Endpoints shows.** It provides “the first bounded implementation and the recurring examples.” Those examples “illustrate the architecture; they do not define its full range,” nor establish that “the registered rule is sound,” that Trust “spans other domains,” or that software “is now safe to ship.”

7. **What remains undecided.** This is a “Draft RFC for feedback,” with “Open decisions and build dependencies.” Crucially, “even supported evidence does not decide whether product policy enables the optimization.”

8. **Likely conclusion.** Trust should answer when evidence justifies “relying on this change, in this domain, under these conditions,” preserve why that answer changes, and automate the lifecycle without turning evidence into “permission to act.”

9. **Likely flattenings.** Readers may mistake it for a proof system, certification regime, safety approval, agent gatekeeper, or standalone product. The specimen rejects each: “not automatically a mathematical proof, certification,” “permission to act,” workflows “do not acquire authority,” and “not a separate product or package roadmap.” They may also mistake the Endpoints example for the whole design, though it “does not define its full range.”
