### Expectedness trace

1. **Suggested argument:** Follow one guarantee through its lifecycle: define, prove, inspect, refresh, reconcile conflict, consume through interfaces, then delegate enforcement decisions.

2. **Invited RFC moves:** Motivation, terminology, end-to-end example, evidence model, trust boundaries, interface surfaces, consumer-policy separation, failure handling, prototype scope, and non-goals.

3. **Dominance:** The use-case spine remains primary because the verbs form a chronological workflow. Architecture and authority enter as boundary explanations. Failure and repair remain supporting rationale. The spine can disappear if each verb becomes an independent capability section.

4. **Nearest generic collapse:** A feature-oriented assurance or provenance platform exposing evidence management through CLI, LSP, and MCP, with downstream policy adapters.

5. **Unpreserved distinctions:** The wording alone cannot hold apart claim, check, and evidence; support and freshness; contradiction, check failure, and staleness; layer authority and consumer policy; human and agent inspection needs; prototype boundaries and worked-consumer status; or illustrative failures and rule-justifying witnesses.

6. **Collapse-risk scene:** The RFC introduces separate “Create,” “Inspect,” “Refresh,” and “Repair” features, then tours equivalent CLI/LSP/MCP commands. No single guarantee persists across those stages, so contradiction becomes another feature and severity or permission becomes a final configuration panel rather than the endpoint of an operational journey.
